What a physical property is

MAT-005 · What a physical property is — Sort a list of characteristics into physical properties and things that are not physical properties.

Script
SayScreen
Two students describe the same metal spoon.[a metal spoon; AVA on the left, BEN on the right]
Ava says the spoon is shiny, hard, and cold to touch.[chips under Ava, one per word: SHINY, HARD, COLD TO TOUCH]
Ava measures the spoon. It is 15 centimeters long, and on a balance it reads 40 grams.[ruler along the spoon: 15 cm; balance readout: 40 g; chips: 15 cm LONG, MASS 40 g]
Ben says the spoon is his favorite, a present, and cost two dollars last year.[chips under Ben: MY FAVORITE, A PRESENT, COST $2, BOUGHT LAST YEAR]
Now imagine a stranger finds the spoon: which facts would still be true?[a stranger picks the spoon up; question mark]
The stranger can see the shine, feel the hardness and the cold, and measure the length and the mass.[ticks appear on Ava's five chips; they slide into a column headed PHYSICAL PROPERTIES]
The stranger cannot tell the owner, the price, or when Ben bought it.[crosses on Ben's four chips]
Those facts are about Ben, not the spoon.[Ben's chips slide into a column headed NOT PHYSICAL PROPERTIES]
Something you can observe or measure about a material, without changing it into anything else, is called a physical property.[chip: PHYSICAL PROPERTY]
Now consider a red rubber ball: anyone can see its color, so color is a physical property.[red ball; chip: RED; tick]
Nobody can tell its owner by looking or measuring, so the owner is not a physical property.[chip: OWNER; cross]
So, here's what we've seen. A physical property is something you can observe or measure about a material without changing it into anything else.[the rule slide: the sorted table, PHYSICAL PROPERTIES and NOT PHYSICAL PROPERTIES; nothing else printed]
So shiny, hard, cold, 15 centimeters and 40 grams are the spoon's physical properties.[the left column highlighted]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “A physical property is something you can observe or measure without changing the material.”]

  1. A The price is a physical property of the spoon.
  2. B The price is not a physical property of the spoon.
Answer

The price is about the owner and the store.

So the price is not a physical property.

Mass: how much material, in grams

MAT-006 · Mass — Identify which of two objects has greater mass given balance readings or a fixed-volume comparison.

Script
SayScreen
An apple on a balance reads 250 grams.[apple on a balance; readout 250 g]
A big bag of feathers on the same balance also reads 250 grams.[apple off; bag of feathers on; readout 250 g]
The bag is far bigger, so does it have more mass?[the two side by side; question mark]
No, it does not.[cross on the question mark]
The balance measures how much material is in the thing on it.[arrow from the pan to the readout]
The amount of material in something is called its mass, measured in grams or kilograms.[chip: MASS; chips: g, kg]
Both read 250 grams, so both have a mass of 250 grams.[chips under both: MASS 250 g]
The bag takes up more room, but how much room a thing takes up is a different property.[dashed outline around the bag; chip: SIZE IS A DIFFERENT PROPERTY]
Now consider two jars the same size: peanut butter and cotton balls.[two identical jars]
The peanut butter reads 500 grams, and the cotton balls read 60 grams.[readouts 500 g and 60 g]
They are the same size, but the peanut butter has far more mass.[chip: SAME SIZE, MORE MASS under the peanut butter]
A small stone at 300 grams has more mass than a big sponge at 50 grams.[stone 300 g; sponge 50 g]
So, mass is the amount of material in something.[the rule slide: the two balances, apple and feathers, both at 250 g; chip MASS; nothing else printed]
A balance measures mass, in grams or kilograms.[the balance readout; chips g, kg]
So the apple and the feathers both read 250 grams, and both have the same mass.[the two balances]
The bag is bigger, but bigger does not mean more mass.[chip: BIGGER IS NOT MORE MASS]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “Mass is the amount of material in something.”]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

Answer

Volume: how much space, in milliliters

MAT-007 · Volume — Identify which of two objects has greater volume given a fixed-mass comparison or a graduated cylinder reading.

Script
SayScreen
Here is a student with a tall thin glass of water and a short wide one.[the two glasses side by side]
The tall glass looks fuller.[chip: LOOKS LIKE MORE over the tall glass]
She pours the tall glass's water into a tall tube with a scale up its side, called a graduated cylinder.[a graduated cylinder; the first pour; chip: GRADUATED CYLINDER]
The water settles at 150 milliliters.[the level rises and settles on the 150 mark]
She empties it and pours in the wide glass's water: this time, 250 milliliters.[cylinder empties; second pour; level settles on the 250 mark]
The wide glass held more, so why were her eyes wrong?[question mark over the two glasses]
Her eyes judged height.[dashed lines at the two water levels]
The amount of space something takes up is called its volume.[chip: VOLUME]
A high level in a thin glass can be a small volume, and a low level in a wide glass a large one.[tall glass: thin column outlined; wide glass: wide pool outlined]
Each numbered mark here is 50 milliliters more than the one below.[zoom on the scale; marks 0 to 300 highlighted in turn; chip: mL]
The small mark halfway between two numbered marks is 25 milliliters more than the lower one.[zoom: the small mark between 200 and 250; label 225 mL]
Let the liquid settle, find its flat top, and read it against the nearest mark.[dashed line across the flat top to the 150 mark]
Write each number with its unit: 150 milliliters and 250 milliliters.[labels: 150 mL, 250 mL]
So, volume is the amount of space something takes up.[the rule slide: the two cylinders at 150 mL and 250 mL; chip VOLUME; nothing else printed]
A graduated cylinder measures the volume of a liquid in milliliters.[the cylinder; chip mL]
So her eyes judged height, and the cylinder measured volume.[the two glasses beside their two cylinders: 150 mL, 250 mL]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “Volume is the amount of space something takes up.”]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

Does it dissolve? Soluble and insoluble

MAT-012 · Solubility as a property — Classify a substance as soluble or insoluble in water based on whether it dissolves.

Script
SayScreen
A student stirs a spoonful of sugar into water.[glass of water; spoon stirs; sugar grains fall in]
The grains disappear, and the water looks clear.[grains shrink to nothing; clear glass]
She stirs a spoonful of sand into a second glass.[second glass; sand falls in; spoon stirs]
The sand sits on the bottom, however long she stirs.[sand swirls, settles; timer runs on]
Why do sugar and sand behave so differently?[question mark between the glasses]
The sugar breaks apart, and the sand does not.[magnifier over each glass]
In the water, the sugar breaks apart into particles far too small to see.[magnifier over the sugar water: a grain breaks into dark circles among light circles; chip: LOOKS CLEAR]
But the water tastes sweet, so the sugar is still there.[chips: TASTES SWEET, STILL THERE]
Breaking into particles too small to see and spreading through the water is called dissolving.[chip: DISSOLVES; dark circles spread through the glass]
A material that dissolves in water is called soluble.[chip: SOLUBLE; tick on the sugar glass]
The sand grains stay whole and sink to the bottom.[magnifier over the sand glass: whole grains on the bottom]
A material that does not dissolve in water is called insoluble.[chip: INSOLUBLE; cross on the sand glass]
Salt disappears too, so salt is soluble.[salt glass clears; tick: SOLUBLE]
Chalk dust clouds the water, then settles, so chalk is insoluble.[cloudy glass clears from the top; chalk on the bottom; cross: INSOLUBLE]
Watching whether a material dissolves does not change it into anything else, so soluble or insoluble is a physical property.[table: soluble | insoluble; chip: PHYSICAL PROPERTY]
So, here's what we've seen. A material that dissolves in water is called soluble.[the rule slide: the two glasses; chip SOLUBLE on the sugar glass; nothing else printed]
A material that does not dissolve in water is called insoluble.[chip INSOLUBLE on the sand glass]
So sugar is soluble, and sand is insoluble.[the two glasses with their labels]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “A material that does not dissolve in water is called insoluble.”]

Conductors and insulators

MAT-013 · Conductors and insulators — Sort materials into thermal and electrical conductors or insulators based on their properties.

Script
SayScreen
A student leaves a steel spoon and a wooden spoon in a pan of hot soup.[pan of soup; two spoons, handles up; labels STEEL, WOOD]
After a minute the steel handle is too hot to hold, but the wooden handle is cool.[timer runs one minute; chips: TOO HOT TO HOLD over the steel; STILL COOL over the wood]
Why did only the steel handle get hot?[question mark]
Steel lets heat through easily, so the hot soup heats the handle too.[arrows climb the steel handle; the handle glows]
Wood hardly lets heat through, so the wooden handle stays cool.[no arrows on the wooden handle]
A material that lets heat through easily is called a conductor: steel is a conductor.[chip: CONDUCTOR under the steel spoon]
A material that hardly lets heat through is called an insulator: wood is an insulator.[chip: INSULATOR under the wooden spoon]
Copper gets hot too, so copper is a conductor.[two more spoons: COPPER with arrows, chip CONDUCTOR]
Plastic stays cool, so plastic is an insulator.[PLASTIC, chip INSULATOR]
For electricity, she puts each material in a simple circuit: if the bulb lights, the material lets electricity through.[chip: ELECTRICITY; battery, bulb, two clips; a gap between the clips]
Copper lights the bulb, and plastic leaves it dark.[copper strip: bulb lights, tick; plastic strip: bulb dark, cross]
Steel lights it too; wood does not.[table fills: steel yes yes; copper yes yes; wood no no; plastic no no]
Steel and copper are metals, and metals conduct both.[table rows highlighted: METALS]
Wood and plastic conduct neither.[table rows highlighted: WOOD and PLASTIC]
So, here's what we've seen. A material that lets heat or electricity pass through easily is called a conductor.[the rule slide: the two spoons and the circuit; chip CONDUCTOR; nothing else printed]
A material that does not is called an insulator.[chip INSULATOR]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “A material that lets heat or electricity pass through easily is called a conductor.”]

  2. B a graduated cylinder
  3. C a thermometer
Answer

Key: B — a graduated cylinder

A graduated cylinder measures the volume of a liquid in milliliters.

Feedback per option

  • A A balance measures mass.
  • B A graduated cylinder measures a liquid's volume, in milliliters.
  • C A thermometer measures temperature.

Which of these is a physical property of a material?

  1. A its price
  2. B who owns it
  3. C whether it dissolves in water
Answer

Key: C — whether it dissolves in water

Whether a material dissolves is something you can observe without changing it into anything else.
So soluble or insoluble is a physical property; the price and the owner are about people.

Feedback per option

  • A A price is about the buyer and the store.
  • B The owner is about a person, not the material.
  • C Anyone can stir the material into water and watch, without changing it into anything else.

A copper strip is put into a simple circuit, and the bulb lights. What is copper?

  1. A a conductor
  2. B an insulator
Answer

Key: A — a conductor

The bulb lights, so copper lets electricity through.
A material that lets electricity pass through easily is called a conductor.

Feedback per option

  • A Copper lets electricity through, so copper is a conductor.
  • B An insulator would leave the bulb dark.
