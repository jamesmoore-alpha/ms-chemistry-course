What an atom is

MAT-042 · What an atom is — State what an atom is.

Script
SayScreen
A student unrolls a strip of aluminum foil and cuts it in half.[a strip of foil; scissors cut it]
She cuts one half in half again, and again, and again.[the strips halve: 1 cut, 2 cuts, 3 cuts, 4 cuts]
The pieces get smaller each time. Could she keep cutting forever?[the smallest strip; question mark]
No, the cutting has an end.[the smallest strip holds]
All matter is made of particles that are far too small to see.[a zoom circle opens on the strip]
After many cuts, each piece is still made of many particles.[zoom: a piece full of gray circles; key chip: GRAY CIRCLE = ALUMINUM ATOM]
Keep cutting in your mind, and in the end one piece is a single aluminum particle.[dots for many cuts; then ONE gray circle]
The smallest piece of aluminum that is still aluminum is called an atom.[chip: ATOM, under the one gray circle]
Every material is made of atoms.[a nail: chip IRON ATOMS; a zoom on air, one joined pair: chip OXYGEN ATOMS]
For aluminum, one particle is one atom.[one gray circle]
For water, one particle is a few atoms joined together.[beside it a group of three circles touching]
In these drawings an atom is a circle. Every atom of one kind has the same size and shade, and the words under the drawing say which kind.[the key: gray circle; large red circle; small pale blue circle; under it the words ONE ATOM OF ALUMINUM, IN THIS DRAWING]
Joined atoms are drawn touching; atoms that are not joined are drawn apart.[a pair touching, labeled JOINED; a pair apart, labeled NOT JOINED]
So, every material is made of atoms. The smallest piece of aluminum that is still aluminum is one atom.[the rule slide: the halving strip ending on the one gray circle; chip ATOM; nothing else printed]
So on the strip, the cutting ends at one atom; she cannot cut forever.[the halving strip ending on the one atom]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “The smallest piece of aluminum that is still aluminum is one atom.”]

Every material is made of atoms.

The smallest piece of aluminum that is still aluminum is one atom.

  • C The cutting ends at one atom, a real piece of aluminum.

One atom is the smallest piece of an element

MAT-042b · Identify the smallest particle of an element — Identify the smallest particle of a given element that still counts as that element.

Script
SayScreen
Here is a copper kettle.[a copper kettle; a zoom circle full of gray circles; key chip: GRAY CIRCLE = COPPER ATOM]
Every material is made of atoms, and the kettle is made of copper atoms.[chip: COPPER ATOMS]
Take one copper atom out of the kettle, in your mind. Is that one atom still copper?[one gray circle lifted out; question mark]
Yes, one copper atom on its own is still copper.[the one gray circle; tick; label STILL COPPER]
One atom is the smallest piece of copper there can be.[label: THE SMALLEST PIECE]
Now imagine a piece even smaller than one atom. Is that piece still copper?[a dashed circle inside the atom; question mark]
No, a piece smaller than one copper atom is not copper anymore.[the dashed circle; cross; label NOT COPPER; chip: COPPER STOPS AT THE ATOM]
Now consider a gold earring: one gold atom is still gold.[earring; one gray circle, key chip: GRAY CIRCLE = GOLD ATOM; tick under STILL GOLD?]
And a hundred gold atoms are still gold.[a scatter of circles, tick]
A piece smaller than one gold atom is not gold.[dashed circle; cross]
Now consider a silver bracelet: one silver atom is still silver.[bracelet; one gray circle, key chip: GRAY CIRCLE = SILVER ATOM; tick under STILL SILVER?]
And anything smaller than one atom is not silver.[dashed circle, cross]
Here's the pattern. One atom of copper is the smallest piece that is still copper; anything smaller is not copper.[the rule slide: the one copper atom with its tick and the dashed circle with its cross; chip COPPER STOPS AT THE ATOM; nothing else printed]
The same is true of gold and silver: the smallest piece that is still that substance is one atom.[the three cases under STILL COPPER?, STILL GOLD?, STILL SILVER?]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “One atom of copper is the smallest piece that is still copper.”]

One kind of atom: an element

MAT-043 · Element — Identify which substances are elements based on whether they contain only one kind of atom.

Script
SayScreen
Here are three drawings of three materials.[three boxes: gray singles; large red pairs; large-open-and-two-dark groups; key chips beside them: GRAY CIRCLE = CARBON ATOM, LARGE RED CIRCLE = OXYGEN ATOM, SMALL PALE BLUE CIRCLE = HYDROGEN ATOM]
In drawing 1 every atom is the same size and shade.[drawing 1 highlighted]
In drawing 2 the atoms sit in pairs, but every atom is still the same kind.[drawing 2 highlighted]
In drawing 3 there are two kinds of atom. Which drawings show only one kind of atom?[drawing 3 highlighted; question mark]
Drawings 1 and 2 do, and drawing 3 does not.[ticks on 1 and 2; cross on 3]
Count the kinds of atom in each drawing.[labels appear one by one: ONE KIND, ONE KIND, TWO KINDS]
A substance made of only one kind of atom is called an element.[chip: ELEMENT]
Drawings 1 and 2 show elements; drawing 3 does not.[ticks on 1 and 2 under ONE KIND OF ATOM?; cross on 3]
Drawing 4, small pale blue circles only, is an element.[drawing 4, tick]
Drawing 5, two kinds not joined, is not an element.[drawing 5, cross]
The oxygen in the air is an element: every atom is an oxygen atom, in joined pairs.[box: OXYGEN, red pairs; tick]
The carbon in charcoal is an element: every atom is a carbon atom.[box: CARBON, gray singles; tick]
Carbon dioxide has carbon atoms and oxygen atoms joined, two kinds, so it is not an element.[box: CARBON DIOXIDE, gray between two open; cross]
So, here's what we've seen. A substance made of only one kind of atom is called an element.[the rule slide: drawings 2 and 3 with their tick and cross; chip ELEMENT; nothing else printed]
So drawings 1 and 2 show elements, and drawing 3 does not.[the three drawings with their verdicts]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “A substance made of only one kind of atom is called an element.”]

Atoms joined together: a molecule

MAT-044 · Molecule — Identify which particle diagrams show molecules based on whether atoms are bonded together.

Script
SayScreen
Two drawings show the same kind of atom.[two boxes of large red circles; key chip: LARGE RED CIRCLE = OXYGEN ATOM]
In drawing 1 each atom floats on its own.[drawing 1: single circles drift apart]
In drawing 2 the atoms sit in pairs, touching, and each pair moves together.[drawing 2: pairs drift as units]
Why does each pair in drawing 2 move as one?[question mark over drawing 2]
The two atoms in each pair are joined together.[one pair highlighted, moving as one]
Atoms that are joined together are drawn touching.[a pair touching; label JOINED]
Two or more atoms joined together make one particle called a molecule.[chip: MOLECULE]
Each pair in drawing 2 is a molecule; the single atoms in drawing 1 are not.[tick on drawing 2 under JOINED ATOMS?; cross on drawing 1]
A molecule can hold atoms of one kind, or atoms of different kinds.[a pair of one kind; a group of two kinds]
The oxygen in the air comes as molecules of two oxygen atoms joined.[zoomed oxygen molecule, atoms labeled]
A water molecule is one oxygen atom joined to two hydrogen atoms.[zoomed water molecule, atoms labeled]
Pairs of one kind, touching, are molecules.[a box of pairs, tick under JOINED ATOMS?]
Single atoms apart are not molecules.[a box of singles, cross]
Pairs of two kinds, touching, are molecules.[a box of two-kind pairs, tick]
Two kinds of single atom apart are not molecules.[a box of two kinds apart, cross]
So, here's what we've seen. Two or more atoms joined together make one particle called a molecule.[the rule slide: drawings 1 and 2 with their cross and tick; chip MOLECULE; nothing else printed]
So the pairs in drawing 2 are molecules, and the single atoms in drawing 1 are not.[drawing 2 tick; drawing 1 cross]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “Two or more atoms joined together make one particle called a molecule.”]

Different atoms joined in a fixed pattern: a compound

MAT-045 · Compound — Identify which substances are compounds based on whether two or more different elements are chemically combined in a fixed ratio.

Script
SayScreen
Here is a glass of water. Water is made of two kinds of atom.[a glass of water; a zoom circle]
Every water molecule has the same pattern: one oxygen atom joined to two hydrogen atoms.[three water molecules; key chips: LARGE RED CIRCLE = OXYGEN ATOM, SMALL PALE BLUE CIRCLE = HYDROGEN ATOM]
Any water molecule anywhere in the world has the same pattern.[a globe; the molecule repeated]
Is water an element?[question mark]
No, water is not an element, because an element has only one kind of atom and water has two.[chip: TWO KINDS OF ATOM; cross under ELEMENT?; NOT AN ELEMENT]
In water, the two kinds of atom are joined into molecules, and the pattern never changes.[the molecule; label ONE PATTERN]
A substance made of two or more kinds of atom joined together in a fixed pattern is called a compound.[chip: COMPOUND]
Water is a compound.[tick under A COMPOUND?]
Carbon dioxide is a compound too: one carbon atom joined to two oxygen atoms, the same in every molecule.[box: gray between two open, nine alike; key chip: GRAY CIRCLE = CARBON ATOM; tick]
Two kinds of atom that are not joined make a mixture, not a compound.[box: two kinds of single atom apart; cross]
One kind of atom, single or joined, makes an element, not a compound.[box: pairs of one kind; cross]
So, here's what we've seen. A substance made of two or more kinds of atom joined together in a fixed pattern is called a compound.[the rule slide: the water box and the mixture box with their tick and cross; chip COMPOUND; nothing else printed]
So water, with two kinds of atom joined in the same pattern every time, is a compound.[the glass; the molecule; A COMPOUND]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “Different kinds of atom joined in a fixed pattern make a compound.”]

Element, compound or mixture from a drawing

MAT-046 · Element or compound — tell them apart using particle diagrams — Sort particle diagrams into elements, compounds, and mixtures using the one-kind-of-atom test.

Script
SayScreen
