What a formula says

MAT-059 · What a chemical formula tells you — Explain what the symbols and subscripts in a chemical formula represent.

Script
SayScreen
A water bottle is labeled H₂O, and a fire extinguisher is labeled CO₂.[the bottle and the extinguisher; the two labels]
Here is one water particle drawn as circles: one large open circle joined to two small dark ones.[the water particle; key: large open = oxygen, small dark = hydrogen]
The label writes the same particle as a letter, a small 2 and another letter.[H₂O beside the particle]
What does each letter say?[the H and the O glow; question mark]
Every element has a chemical symbol, so H is hydrogen and O is oxygen.[H — hydrogen; O — oxygen]
Each atom lines up under its own letter.[the two hydrogen atoms slide under the H; the oxygen atom slides under the O]
And what does the small 2 count?[the 2 glows; question mark]
It sits low after the H and counts the atoms of the element just before it: 2 hydrogen atoms.[the 2 boxed; the two small dark circles ringed]
A small number written low after a symbol is called a subscript.[chip: SUBSCRIPT]
The O has no subscript, so the O counts one oxygen atom.[the O; label: 1 oxygen atom]
This way of writing a substance is called its chemical formula.[chip: CHEMICAL FORMULA; H₂O = 2 hydrogen atoms + 1 oxygen atom]
Now consider CO₂: C is carbon and O is oxygen.[CO₂ beside its particle: the gray circle onto the C, the two large open circles onto the O]
The 2 after the O counts 2 oxygen atoms.[the 2 boxed; the two large open circles ringed]
So, a chemical formula writes one particle of a substance.[the rule slide: H₂O and CO₂ with their particles; chips CHEMICAL FORMULA, SUBSCRIPT, O = 1 oxygen atom; nothing else printed]
Each symbol names one element in the particle, and a subscript counts the atoms of the element just before it.[the symbols and the subscripts glow in turn]
So each of the two labels writes one particle.[the bottle and the extinguisher; the particles beside the labels]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: one line — “A chemical formula writes one particle of a substance.”]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

video

A water bottle is labeled H₂O. What does the small 2 count?

  1. A hydrogen atoms
  2. B oxygen atoms
  3. C water particles
Answer

Key: A — hydrogen atoms

A subscript counts the atoms of the element just before it.
The 2 comes just after the H.
So the 2 counts 2 hydrogen atoms.

Feedback per option

  • A The 2 sits just after the H, so it counts hydrogen atoms: 2 hydrogen atoms.
  • B The 2 comes after the H, not the O; the O with no subscript is 1 oxygen atom.
  • C A subscript counts atoms in one particle, not particles.

Q1recall

A label reads KBr. How many elements does KBr name?

  1. A one element
  2. B two elements
Answer

Key: B — two elements

Every capital letter starts a new chemical symbol.
KBr has two capital letters: the K and the B.
So KBr is two symbols, potassium and bromine: two elements.

Feedback per option

  • A KBr has two capital letters, K and B, so it names two elements: potassium and bromine.
  • B Every capital letter starts a new symbol. KBr has two capitals, K and B: two elements, potassium and bromine.
Q2recall

Every particle of a substance holds two kinds of atom joined together in a fixed pattern. What kind of substance is it?

  1. A a compound
  2. B an element
  3. C a mixture
Answer

Key: A — a compound

The particles hold two kinds of atom.
The two kinds are joined together in a fixed pattern.
A substance made of two or more kinds of atom joined together in a fixed pattern is called a compound.

Feedback per option

  • A Two or more kinds of atom joined together in a fixed pattern make a compound.
  • B An element has only one kind of atom; this substance has two kinds joined.
  • C In a mixture the substances are mixed together, not joined; here the two kinds of atom are joined.

Count the atoms in a formula

MAT-060 · Count atoms in a simple formula — Count the number of atoms of each element in a formula that contains only subscripts.

Script
SayScreen
A food-science book lists glucose, the sugar in grapes, as C₆H₁₂O₆.[the book page; C₆H₁₂O₆ large]
One glucose particle is built from many atoms: how many of each element, and how many in all?[four blanks: C ?, H ?, O ?, all ?]
The formula tells you, in three steps.[1, 2, 3 as bold numbers]
A formula writes one particle, and each subscript counts that element's atoms.[H₂O with its particle; the 2 boxed]
One: take the symbols one at a time: C, then H, then O.[C, H, O light up in turn]
Two: read the subscript after each symbol: that subscript is that element's count.[6, 12, 6 boxed in turn]
C₆ is 6 carbon atoms, H₁₂ is 12 hydrogen atoms, and O₆ is 6 oxygen atoms.[table rows fill: carbon 6, hydrogen 12, oxygen 6]
Three: add the counts for the total: 6 plus 12 plus 6 is 24 atoms in one glucose particle.[total row: 24]
Now consider methane, CH₄: the C with no subscript is 1 carbon atom.[CH₄ beside its particle; table row: carbon 1]
The 4 after the H is 4 hydrogen atoms: 5 atoms in all.[the 4 boxed; table row: hydrogen 4; total 5]
So, to count the atoms in a formula, take the symbols one at a time, read each subscript as that element's count, and add the counts for the total.[the rule slide: the glucose counting table; nothing else printed]
So glucose is 6 carbon atoms, 12 hydrogen atoms and 6 oxygen atoms: 24 atoms in one particle.[the four blanks filled]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: bullets — • Take the symbols one at a time • Read each subscript as that element's count • Add the counts for the total]

  2. B 12
  3. C 24
Answer

Feedback per option

  • B 12 is the hydrogen count alone; the total adds all three.
