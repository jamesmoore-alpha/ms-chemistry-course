A mixture made by dissolving: a solution

MAT-033 · Solution — Identify which mixtures are solutions based on whether one substance has dissolved evenly into another.

Script
SayScreen
A student stirs a packet of drink mix into a glass of water.[a glass of water; powder pours in; a spoon stirs]
The drink mix vanishes, and the water turns the same color all the way through.[the glass turns one even shade]
She stirs flour into a second glass; it turns cloudy, then the flour settles.[a second glass clouds; a layer forms at the bottom]
Both glasses hold mixtures, so why did only the drink mix seem to disappear?[both glasses; question mark over the first]
The drink mix dissolved, and the flour did not.[chip over the first glass: DISSOLVED]
Zoomed in on the drink mix glass, its particles are spread evenly among the water particles.[sample box: open drink mix particles spread among filled water particles]
Zoomed in on the flour glass, the flour particles are still stuck together in grains on the bottom.[sample box: clumps of open particles on the floor]
A mixture in which one substance has dissolved evenly into another is called a solution.[chip: SOLUTION]
Salt stirred into water vanishes and spreads evenly, so salt water is a solution.[question line A SOLUTION? above the cases; salt box; tick]
Pepper shaken into water floats and sinks and never vanishes, so it is not a solution.[pepper box with grains; cross]
Vinegar poured into water vanishes into it, so it is a solution, with no solid at all.[vinegar box; tick]
So, here's what we've seen. A mixture in which one substance has dissolved evenly into another is called a solution.[the rule slide: the drink mix box and the flour box with their tick and cross; chip SOLUTION; nothing else printed]
So the drink mix glass holds a solution, and the flour glass does not.[the two glasses; tick on the first, cross on the second]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “A mixture in which one substance has dissolved evenly into another is called a solution.”]

Drink mix stirred into one glass of water vanishes and spreads evenly through it; flour stirred into another glass settles. Which glass holds a solution?

  1. A the drink mix glass
  2. B the flour glass
  3. C both glasses
Answer

Key: A — the drink mix glass

A mixture in which one substance has dissolved evenly into another is called a solution.
The drink mix dissolves evenly; the flour stays as grains.
So only the drink mix glass holds a solution.

Feedback per option

  • B The flour stays as grains and settles, so it does not dissolve.
  • C Only the drink mix dissolves. The flour glass holds a mixture that is not a solution.

The two parts of a solution: solute and solvent

MAT-034 · Solute and solvent — Label the solute and solvent in a given solution.

Script
SayScreen
A recipe card for hummingbird nectar reads: dissolve fifty grams of sugar in two hundred milliliters of water.[the recipe card]
Two substances make this solution: one dissolves, and the other does the dissolving.[SUGAR and WATER highlighted on the card]
Which is which?[question mark]
The sugar dissolves, and the water does the dissolving.[arrow from SUGAR: dissolves; arrow from WATER: does the dissolving]
The sugar breaks into particles too small to see and spreads out among the water particles.[sample box: open sugar particles spreading among filled water particles]
The water spreads the sugar particles out, so the water does the dissolving.[the water particles jostle the sugar particles apart]
The substance that dissolves is called the solute.[chip: SOLUTE, under the sugar]
The substance the solute dissolves into is called the solvent.[chip: SOLVENT, under the water]
Solute plus solvent makes a solution.[solute + solvent → solution]
The solvent is usually a liquid, and there is usually more of it.[200 mL of water beside 50 g of sugar]
In soda water, the carbon dioxide gas is the solute, and the water is the solvent.[bottle; labels SOLUTE: gas, SOLVENT: water]
In sweet tea with honey, the honey is the solute, and the tea is the solvent.[cup; labels]
With food coloring in water, the coloring is the solute, and the water is the solvent.[jar; labels]
So, the substance that dissolves is called the solute. The substance it dissolves into is called the solvent.[the rule slide: the recipe card; chips SOLUTE under the sugar, SOLVENT under the water; nothing else printed]
So in the recipe, the sugar is the solute, and the water is the solvent.[the card with both labels]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “The substance that dissolves is called the solute.”]

How concentrated? More solute in the same solvent

MAT-035 · Concentration — Order solutions from most to least concentrated given the amounts of solute and solvent in each.

Script
SayScreen
Three mugs each hold two hundred milliliters of hot water.[three mugs; label 200 mL on each]
A student stirs five grams of instant coffee into the first, ten grams into the second, twenty grams into the third.[5 g, 10 g, 20 g spoon in; each stirred]
All the coffee dissolves, but the third mug is darkest and tastes strongest.[the mugs shade pale, darker, darkest]
What is different about the third mug?[question mark over the third mug]
It holds the most coffee in the same water.[chip over the third mug: MOST COFFEE, SAME WATER]
In every mug the coffee is the solute and the water is the solvent.[labels SOLUTE: coffee, SOLVENT: water]
The water is the same in every mug, but the third holds four times the coffee.[table: 5 g, 10 g, 20 g against 200 mL, 200 mL, 200 mL]
Each sip from the third mug carries more coffee, so it tastes stronger and looks darker.[a sip from each mug; more open particles in the third]
How much solute is dissolved in a given amount of solvent is called the concentration.[chip: CONCENTRATION]
More solute in the same solvent means more concentrated.[the table gains a column: most … least concentrated]
Two pitchers each hold thirty grams of sugar: one in a thousand milliliters of water, one in five hundred milliliters.[two pitchers; 30 g in each; 1,000 mL and 500 mL]
The pitcher with five hundred milliliters has the same sugar in less water, so it is more concentrated.[the 500 mL pitcher highlighted: MORE CONCENTRATED]
The same solute in more solvent means less concentrated.[the 1,000 mL pitcher: LESS CONCENTRATED]
So, more solute in the same solvent means more concentrated. The same solute in more solvent means less concentrated.[the rule slide: the mugs table with its most-to-least column; chip CONCENTRATION; nothing else printed]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “More solute in the same solvent means more concentrated.”]

Adding water: diluting a solution

MAT-036 · Dilution — Predict what happens to the concentration of a solution when more solvent is added.

Script
SayScreen
A glass holds a dark, concentrated drink: forty grams of syrup in two hundred milliliters of water.[a dark glass; label 40 g of syrup, 200 mL of water]
A student tops the glass up to four hundred milliliters with more water.[water pours in; the level rises to a 400 mL line]
The color pales and the drink tastes weaker, yet no syrup was taken out.[the glass pales]
Why does the drink taste weaker?[question mark]
The same syrup is now spread through twice the water.[label 40 g in 400 mL]
The syrup is the solute and the water is the solvent.[labels SOLUTE: syrup, SOLVENT: water]
Before the extra water, zoomed in, the syrup particles sit close together among the water particles.[sample box: six open syrup particles among ten filled water particles]
After the water is added, every syrup particle is still there, spread through twice the water.[the box stretches to twice its width; the same six open particles spread out]
Each sip is the same size, but it carries fewer syrup particles.[a sip-sized circle over each box; fewer open particles inside the second]
So it tastes weaker, because the concentration has gone down.[chip: LESS CONCENTRATED]
Adding more solvent to a solution is called diluting it.[chip: DILUTING]
Diluting takes no solute away: all forty grams stay in the glass.[40 g stays on the label]
Salty soup filled up with water is diluted.[a pot; water pours in; label DILUTED]
Tea with more hot water poured in is diluted too.[a cup of tea pales; label DILUTED]
Adding more syrup instead makes the drink more concentrated, and that is not diluting.[syrup pours in; the glass darkens; cross over DILUTING]
So, adding more solvent to a solution is called diluting it, and the concentration goes down.[the rule slide: the before-and-after boxes; chip DILUTING; nothing else printed]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “Adding more solvent to a solution is called diluting it, and the concentration goes down.”]

Mass adds up when a solution forms

MAT-037 · Mass is conserved when a solution forms — Calculate the total mass of a solution from the masses of solute and solvent before mixing.

Script
SayScreen
A student puts an empty beaker on a balance and sets the reading to zero, then pours in water until it reads two hundred grams.[an empty beaker on a balance: readout 0 g; water pours in; readout 200 g]
She measures fifteen grams of salt onto a paper.[a paper with a heap on a second balance; 15 g]
She tips the salt in and stirs until every grain has vanished.[the heap slides in; the spoon stirs; the grains disappear]
She puts the beaker back on the balance. What does the salt water read?[the beaker returns; readout blank; question mark]
It reads two hundred fifteen grams, and here is why.[readout 215 g]
Zoomed in, the salt particles are spread out among the water particles.[sample box: open salt particles spread among filled water particles]
Every salt particle is still in the beaker, so its mass is still there.[the box outline; nothing crosses it]
The mass of a solution equals the mass of the solvent plus the mass of the solute.[equation: mass of solution = mass of solvent + mass of solute]
Two hundred grams plus fifteen grams equals two hundred fifteen grams.[worked form fills line by line; readout 215 g]
Write the values, the equation, the substitution, the calculation, and the answer with its unit.[the five line labels appear in turn]
Twelve grams of baking soda in two hundred fifty grams of water makes two hundred sixty-two grams.[worked form for the baking soda; 262 g]
Milliliters measure volume, not mass, so three hundred milliliters of water cannot go into the sum; only grams are added.[300 mL crossed out of a sum]
So, the mass of a solution equals the mass of the solvent plus the mass of the solute.[the rule slide: the equation card mass of solution = mass of solvent + mass of solute above the three balances; nothing else printed]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “The mass of a solution equals the mass of the solvent plus the mass of the solute.”]

Every salt particle is still in the beaker.

The topic’s fixed wording (the lexicon the scripts repeat)
  • A mixture in which one substance has dissolved evenly into another is called a solution.
  • The substance that dissolves is called the solute. The substance it dissolves into is called the solvent.
  • How much solute is dissolved in a given amount of solvent is called the concentration.
  • Adding more solvent to a solution is called diluting it, and the concentration goes down.
  • The mass of a solution equals the mass of the solvent plus the mass of the solute.
  • Warmer water, smaller pieces and stirring each make a solute dissolve faster.
  • To separate a mixture, find a property that one part has and the other does not.
