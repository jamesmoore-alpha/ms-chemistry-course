Find the volume of an odd-shaped solid

MAT-008 · Find the volume of an irregular solid by displacement — Calculate the volume of an irregular solid from the start and end readings of a graduated cylinder.

Script
SayScreen
A student wants the volume of a small stone.[a lumpy stone; the student]
The stone is lumpy, so a ruler is no use.[a ruler held against the stone; cross]
She has a graduated cylinder, a tall tube marked off in steps, holding forty milliliters of water.[the cylinder; scale 0 to 100 mL; level at 40]
How can the water tell her the stone's volume?[question mark]
The water will show the space the stone takes up.[the water level highlighted]
She lowers the stone in, and the whole stone goes under.[the stone sinks to the bottom]
The water level rises and settles at fifty-five milliliters.[the level climbs from 40 to 55; label 55 mL]
The stone takes up space, so it pushes the water out of its way.[zoom: the stone at the bottom; small arrows push water upward]
The level rose by exactly the stone's own volume.[bracket from 40 to 55]
Pushing the water aside like this is called displacement.[chip: DISPLACEMENT]
Fifty-five milliliters minus forty milliliters is fifteen milliliters: the stone displaced fifteen milliliters of water.[55 mL − 40 mL = 15 mL]
One milliliter of water fills a cube one centimeter on each side, so one milliliter is one cubic centimeter.[a 1 cm cube; 1 mL of water pours in and fills it; 1 mL = 1 cm³]
So the stone's volume is fifteen cubic centimeters.[chip: 15 cm³]
Now consider a lump of clay. The level rises from forty milliliters to seventy milliliters, so the clay's volume is thirty cubic centimeters.[clay lump; level 40 to 70; 30 cm³]
So, volume of the solid equals end reading minus start reading.[the rule slide: the two cylinders at 40 mL and 55 mL with the bracket between; nothing else printed]
So for the stone, forty milliliters to fifty-five milliliters gives fifteen cubic centimeters.[the stone's two cylinders; 15 cm³]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “Volume of the solid equals end reading minus start reading.”]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

video

A stone goes under the water in a graduated cylinder, and the level rises from 40 mL to 55 mL. What is the stone's volume?

  1. A 15 cm³
  2. B 40 cm³
  3. C 55 cm³
Answer

Key: A — 15 cm³

Volume of the solid equals end reading minus start reading.
55 mL − 40 mL = 15 mL.
Each 1 mL displaced means 1 cm³ of solid, so the stone's volume is 15 cm³.

Feedback per option

  • A 55 mL − 40 mL = 15 mL, and each 1 mL displaced means 1 cm³ of solid.
  • B 40 mL is the start reading, the water alone.
  • C 55 mL is the end reading, the water and the stone together.

Which is denser?

MAT-009 · What density is — Decide which of two objects is denser given their mass and volume, without calculating.

Script
SayScreen
Two blocks sit on a table, exactly the same size: one steel, one oak.[two blocks side by side; labels STEEL, OAK]
A student lifts them, and the steel block is far harder to lift.[a hand lifts each; the steel one barely moves]
On a balance, the steel reads four hundred grams and the oak thirty-five grams.[two balances: 400 g, 35 g]
They are the same size but not the same mass, so what is different about the steel?[question mark]
It packs more mass into the same space.[the steel block highlighted]
Each block has a volume of fifty cubic centimeters.[labels: 50 cm³ under each block]
The steel packs four hundred grams into that space; the oak packs only thirty-five grams.[chips: 400 g IN 50 cm³; 35 g IN 50 cm³]
So the steel packs far more mass into each cubic centimeter.[zoom on one 1 cm cube of each block]
How much mass a material packs into each cubic centimeter is called its density.[chip: DENSITY]
Same volume and more mass means denser: steel is denser than oak.[verdict: STEEL IS DENSER]
Now consider a steel ball and a glass ball that both read forty grams.[two balances, both 40 g; small dark ball, bigger clear ball]
The steel ball is five cubic centimeters; the glass ball is sixteen cubic centimeters.[labels: 5 cm³, 16 cm³]
The steel packs the same mass into less space, so the steel ball is denser.[verdict: STEEL IS DENSER]
So, here's what we've seen. Same volume and more mass means denser.[the rule slide: the table of the two pairs; the blocks row highlighted; nothing else printed]
Same mass and less volume means denser.[the balls row highlighted]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: bullets — • Same volume and more mass means denser. • Same mass and less volume means denser.]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

video

A steel block and an oak block are the same size. The steel reads 400 g and the oak 35 g. Which is denser?

  1. A The oak block is denser.
  2. B The two blocks have the same density.
  3. C The steel block is denser.
Answer

Key: C — The steel block is denser.

The two blocks have the same volume.
The steel block has far more mass: 400 g against 35 g.
Same volume and more mass means denser, so the steel block is denser.

Feedback per option

  • A The oak has far less mass in the same volume.
  • B The volumes match, but the masses differ.
  • C Same volume, and the steel has far more mass.

Calculate density

MAT-010 · Calculate density — Calculate the density of a substance from its mass and volume, with correct units.

Script
SayScreen
A steel cube has a mass of forty-eight grams and a volume of six cubic centimeters.[steel cube; chips: 48 g, 6 cm³]
An oak block has a mass of fifty-six grams and a volume of eighty cubic centimeters.[oak block; chips: 56 g, 80 cm³]
Neither the masses nor the volumes match, so comparing cannot decide.[the two pairs of chips; cross over the comparison]
How dense is each one, as a number?[question mark]
The steel packs forty-eight grams into six cubic centimeters.[the cube split into six 1 cm cubes]
If we share the forty-eight grams equally between the six cubic centimeters, each one gets eight grams.[8 g lands on each small cube]
So the steel's density is eight grams per cubic centimeter.[chip: 8 g/cm³; the unit spelled out beneath]
To find a density, divide the mass by the volume: density equals mass divided by volume.[equation: density = mass ÷ volume, terms labelled]
Now for the oak, fifty-six grams divided by eighty cubic centimeters gives zero point seven grams per cubic centimeter.[worked form for the oak fills line by line]
Now consider an aluminum bar: eighty-one grams divided by thirty cubic centimeters gives two point seven grams per cubic centimeter.[aluminum bar; worked form; 2.7 g/cm³]
Every time, write the values, the equation, the substitution, the calculation, and the answer with its unit.[the five line labels appear in turn]
So, density equals mass divided by volume, in grams per cubic centimeter.[the rule slide: the equation card density = mass ÷ volume with its key; nothing else printed]
So the steel at eight grams per cubic centimeter is denser than the oak at zero point seven grams per cubic centimeter.[table: steel 8 g/cm³, oak 0.7 g/cm³]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “Density equals mass divided by volume, in grams per cubic centimeter.”]

A steel cube packs 48 g into 6 cm³. What is the steel's density?

  1. A 8 g/cm³
  2. B 42 g/cm³
  3. C 288 g/cm³
Answer

Key: A — 8 g/cm³

Density equals mass divided by volume.
48 g ÷ 6 cm³ = 8 g/cm³.

Feedback per option

  • A 48 g ÷ 6 cm³ = 8 g/cm³.
  • B 48 − 6 = 42, but density is mass divided by volume, not mass minus volume.
  • C 48 × 6 = 288, but density is mass divided by volume, not mass times volume.

MAT-015 · Characteristic properties do not depend on the amount of a substance — Explain why the density of a small sample and a large sample of the same substance are identical.

SayScreen

No, it is not.[cross on the question mark]
