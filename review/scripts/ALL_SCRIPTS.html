In a chemical reaction the atoms let go of their old partners and join new ones. So new substances form.

The substances that go into a reaction are called the reactants, and the substances that come out are called the products.

A word equation writes the reactants on the left, an arrow that reads “react to make”, and the products on the right.

To find every element in a reaction, read each symbol, look it up on the periodic table, and write each element once.

The total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants: this is the law of conservation of mass.

To find a missing mass, write the law in the reaction's own substances, make the unknown the subject, substitute, and calculate.

In an open container a gas made by the reaction floats away, so the balance reads less. No mass was destroyed.

An equation obeys the law of conservation of mass when each kind of atom counts the same on the left and on the right.

Plants build glucose from carbon dioxide and water. So a tree's new mass comes from the air and the water, not the soil.
