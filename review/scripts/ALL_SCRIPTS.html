Solid, liquid or gas?

MAT-016 · Solid, liquid or gas — tell them apart — Classify a sample as a solid, a liquid or a gas from whether it keeps its own shape and whether it keeps its own volume.

Dry sand pours into a jar and takes its shape, just as water would.

So is sand a liquid?

Ask two questions: does it keep its own shape, and does it keep its own volume?

Now 250 milliliters of juice.

In a tall glass it stands tall. In a wide bowl it lies flat.

A measuring cup still reads 250 milliliters.

It takes its container's shape but keeps its volume, so the juice is a liquid.

Now 20 milliliters of air sealed in a syringe.

Pull the plunger out, and the air fills 60 milliliters.

Push it in, and the same air fits into 10 milliliters.

It keeps neither shape nor volume, so the air is a gas.

A solid keeps its shape and its volume.

A gas keeps neither. It spreads to fill whatever space it is given.
