Acids: the sour family

MAT-077 · Acid — Identify a substance as an acid from its observable properties or indicator results.

Script
SayScreen
Lemon juice, vinegar and soda water all taste sour.[three beakers: lemon juice, vinegar, soda water]
A strip of blue paper dipped into each one turns red.[a hatched strip dips into each; the dipped end turns dark gray; legend: hatched = blue litmus paper, dark gray = red]
Three liquids give one color change, so something about them is the same.[the three beakers; the three red ends]
The strip is paper soaked in a dye that can change color, and it is called litmus paper.[one strip, close up; chip: LITMUS PAPER]
In vinegar, the dipped end turns red.[vinegar beaker; the strip's dipped end turns dark gray]
In lemon juice, the dipped end turns red.[lemon juice beaker; same change]
In pure water, the paper stays blue.[pure water beaker; the strip stays hatched]
In soda water, the dipped end turns red.[soda water beaker; turns dark gray]
In soapy water, the paper stays blue.[soapy water beaker; stays hatched]
The three sour liquids turned blue litmus paper red; the other two left it blue.[five beakers in a row; question: Does blue litmus turn red? ticks under three, crosses under two]
A substance that tastes sour and turns blue litmus paper red is called an acid.[chip: ACID over the three ticked beakers]
The paper changed color to show each liquid's family. A substance that does this is called an indicator.[chip: INDICATOR over a strip]
Vinegar and lemon juice are acids in food. The sulfuric acid in a car battery burns skin.[a car battery with a warning label]
So in a lab the test for an acid is litmus paper: never taste, never touch.[a strip; a crossed-out tongue and a crossed-out finger]
So, here's what we've seen. Acids taste sour.[the rule slide: the three ticked beakers; chip ACID; nothing else printed]
And acids turn blue litmus paper red.[the three red strip ends]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: bullets — • Acids taste sour • Acids turn blue litmus paper red]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

video

Blue litmus paper is dipped into vinegar. What does the dipped end do?

  1. A it turns red
  2. B it stays blue
  3. C it turns green
Answer

Key: A — it turns red

Vinegar tastes sour and is an acid.
Acids turn blue litmus paper red.
So the dipped end turns red.

Feedback per option

  • A Vinegar is an acid, and acids turn blue litmus paper red.
  • B The paper stays blue in pure water and soapy water; in vinegar the dipped end turns red.
  • C Litmus paper turns red or blue, never green; in vinegar it turns red.

Which of these is a physical property of vinegar?

  1. A It tastes sour.
  2. B It was bought on a Tuesday.
  3. C It belongs to the cook.
Answer

Key: A — It tastes sour.

A physical property is something you can observe or measure about a material without changing it into anything else.
You can taste vinegar and observe that it is sour.
So its sour taste is a physical property.

Feedback per option

Bases: the slippery family

MAT-078 · Base — Identify a substance as a base from its observable properties or indicator results.

Script
SayScreen
Soapy water feels slippery between your fingers, and so does washing soda solution.[two beakers: soapy water, washing soda solution; a hand rubs fingers]
Red litmus paper dipped into each one turns blue.[dark gray strips dip; the dipped ends turn hatched; legend: hatched = blue litmus paper, dark gray = red]
Acids turn blue litmus paper red, and these liquids do the opposite.[a blue strip turning red beside a red strip turning blue]
Soapy water is slippery and bitter, and the dipped end turns blue.[soapy water beaker; strip's dipped end turns hatched; labels: slippery, bitter]
Washing soda solution is slippery, and the dipped end turns blue.[washing soda solution; same change]
Vinegar is sour, not slippery, and the paper stays red.[vinegar; strip stays dark gray]
Ammonia cleaner is slippery, and the dipped end turns blue.[ammonia cleaner; turns hatched]
Pure water has no taste, is not slippery, and the paper stays red.[pure water; stays dark gray]
The three slippery liquids turned red litmus paper blue; vinegar and pure water left it red.[five beakers; question: Does red litmus turn blue? ticks under three, crosses under two]
A substance that feels slippery, tastes bitter and turns red litmus paper blue is called a base.[chip: BASE over the three ticked beakers]
Acids and bases are opposite families: an acid turns blue litmus paper red, and a base turns red litmus paper blue.[the acid-and-base table: taste, feel, blue litmus, red litmus]
The sodium hydroxide in drain cleaner is a strong base. It burns skin.[a drain cleaner bottle with a warning label]
So in a lab no liquid is touched or tasted; the test is litmus paper.[a strip; crossed-out finger and tongue]
So, here's what we've seen. Bases feel slippery.[the rule slide: the three ticked beakers; chip BASE; nothing else printed]
Bases taste bitter.[label: bitter, by the soapy water]
Bases turn red litmus paper blue.[the three blue strip ends]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: bullets — • Bases feel slippery • Bases taste bitter • Bases turn red litmus paper blue]

The pH scale

MAT-079 · The pH scale — Classify a substance as acidic, neutral, or basic given its pH value, using water as the neutral reference point.

Script
SayScreen
Lemon juice and soda water are both acids, yet lemon juice tastes far more sour.[two beakers; a screwed-up face over the lemon juice]
A meter gives a liquid a number: lemon juice reads 2, soda water 4, pure water 7.[three meters; readouts 2, 4, 7]
What does the number tell you?[question mark]
It says how acidic or basic the liquid is.[the three readouts; chip: HOW ACIDIC?]
The meter's number, from 0 to 14, is called the pH of the liquid.[chip: pH; a bare line from 0 to 14 appears]
Pure water sits in the middle at pH 7, and a liquid at pH 7 is called neutral.[marker at 7: pure water; chip: NEUTRAL]
The acids sit below 7: lemon juice at 2, vinegar at 3, soda water at 4.[markers slide onto 2, 3, 4]
A liquid below 7 is called acidic, and the lower the pH, the more acidic it is.[arrow pointing left under the line: more acidic; chip: ACIDIC]
The bases sit above 7: baking soda solution at 8, soapy water at 10, ammonia cleaner at 11.[markers slide onto 8, 10, 11]
A liquid above 7 is called basic, and the higher the pH, the more basic it is.[arrow pointing right: more basic; chip: BASIC]
This line from 0 to 14 is called the pH scale. Its 0 end is the most acidic end, not no acid.[chip: THE pH SCALE over the whole line; label at 0: most acidic]
The pH scale runs from 0 to 14.[the rule slide: the scale with its markers; chips ACIDIC, NEUTRAL, BASIC; nothing else printed]
Pure water sits in the middle, at pH 7.[the marker at 7 glows]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: bullets — • The pH scale runs from 0 to 14 • Pure water: pH 7, neutral • Below 7: acidic; the lower, the more acidic • Above 7: basic; the higher, the more basic]
