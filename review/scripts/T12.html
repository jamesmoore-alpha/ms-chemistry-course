Where the new substance's atoms come from

MAT-068 · Atoms are rearranged in a reaction, not created or destroyed — Explain where the atoms in the products of a reaction come from.

Script

Nobody added anything: where did the extra 1.2 g come from?[question mark over the readout]

The rule: the atoms in a new substance come from the substances that were there before.[rule slide reuses the rust boxes; rule text]
Back to the pad: the extra 1.2 g is the oxygen atoms that joined on.[the pad on the balance; 11.2 g]

A chemical reaction rearranges atoms

MAT-066 · Chemical reaction — Identify a chemical reaction by recognising that atoms are rearranged to form new substances.

Script
SayScreen
Here are three changes drawn before and after: ice melting, sugar dissolving, and hydrogen burning in oxygen.[three before-and-after pairs in a row]
Only one makes a new substance: which one?[question mark over the three pairs]
The atoms in a new substance come from the substances that were there before.[chip: SAME ATOMS]
Watch hydrogen burn: each pair of hydrogen atoms lets go, and each pair of oxygen atoms lets go.[the pairs separate in the before box]
Now each oxygen atom joins two hydrogen atoms: a water molecule, a new substance.[the atoms regroup into four water molecules; label: water]
Count the circles: the same 8 hydrogen and 4 oxygen atoms before and after.[counts appear under both boxes]
Now watch ice melt: the water molecules move, but every atom keeps its partners.[the same five molecules drift; no atom lets go; cross]
A change in which atoms rearrange into new substances is called a chemical reaction.[chip: CHEMICAL REACTION over the hydrogen pair]
Now consider charcoal burning: each carbon atom joins two oxygen atoms, a chemical reaction.[carbon and oxygen pairs regroup into carbon dioxide; tick]
And sugar dissolving: the sugar molecules spread among the water molecules, still whole: not a reaction.[sugar molecules drift apart among water molecules; cross]
Each reaction goes on one line: hydrogen plus oxygen makes water.[strip: hydrogen + oxygen → water]
So here is the rule: in a chemical reaction the atoms let go of their old partners and join new ones.[rule slide reuses the hydrogen pair; rule text]
Back to the three drawings: only hydrogen burning shows atoms changing partners.[the third pair comes forward; tick]
Okay, now your turn.[the attached question appears]

  1. A ice melting

Answer

Key: C — hydrogen burning

In hydrogen burning, the hydrogen pairs and the oxygen pairs let go.

The atoms in a new substance come from the substances that were there before.[the rust pair: 4 and 4 under each box]
In a chemical reaction the atoms let go of their old partners and join new ones. So new substances form.[the hydrogen pair; chip: CHEMICAL REACTION]
Melting and dissolving move the particles around without changing them. So they are not chemical reactions.[the ice pair and the sugar pair; crosses]
The substances that go into a reaction are called the reactants, and the substances that come out are called the products.[the labeled methane strip]
A word equation writes the reactants on the left, an arrow that reads “react to make”, and the products on the right.[the strip; the arrow glows]
To find every element in a reaction, read each symbol, look it up on the periodic table, and write each element once.[the formula form with symbols underlined; the table]
The total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants: this is the law of conservation of mass.[the lidded jar: 250.0 g and 250.0 g]
To find a missing mass, write the law in the reaction's own substances, make the unknown the subject, substitute, and calculate.[the worked block; 10.0 g − 5.6 g = 4.4 g]
In an open container a gas made by the reaction floats away, so the balance reads less. No mass was destroyed.[the open dish; the reading falls to 5.6 g; the gas leaving]
An equation obeys the law of conservation of mass when each kind of atom counts the same on the left and on the right.[the hydrogen count strip; the table of matches]
Plants build glucose from carbon dioxide and water. So a tree's new mass comes from the air and the water, not the soil.[the two tubs; the photosynthesis strip]

video

The summary showed the limestone in the open dish. What did it say happened to the missing 4.4 g?

  1. A it was destroyed by the heat
  2. B it soaked into the dish's walls
  3. C it left as carbon dioxide gas
Answer

Key: C — it left as carbon dioxide gas

The reaction made 4.4 g of carbon dioxide gas.
The dish was open, so the gas floated away into the air.
So the missing 4.4 g left as carbon dioxide gas.

Feedback per option

  • A No mass is destroyed in a reaction; the summary said the gas floated away.
  • B Nothing soaked in; the summary said the carbon dioxide gas floated out of the open dish.
  • C The reaction made carbon dioxide gas, and the open dish let it float away with its mass.
