One kind of matter: a pure substance

MAT-027 · Pure substance — Identify which samples are pure substances based on whether they contain only one kind of matter.

Script
SayScreen
A student pours a glass of distilled water, the kind sold for steam irons, and a glass of orange juice.[two glasses side by side; labels DISTILLED WATER, ORANGE JUICE]
After an hour the water is the same all through, but the juice has settled, with pulp at the bottom.[a clock hand sweeps one hour; the water glass even all through; the juice glass with a pulp layer]
What is different about the water?[question mark over the water glass]
The water holds only one kind of matter.[chip over the water glass: ONE KIND OF MATTER]
All matter is made of particles that are far too small to see.[a zoom circle opens on the water]
Zoomed in, every particle in the distilled water is a water particle.[sample box: sixteen filled circles; legend WATER PARTICLES]
Zoomed in, the juice holds water particles, sugar particles and pulp, all mingled.[sample box: filled and open circles mingled; legend WATER, SUGAR, AND MORE]
A sample that contains only one kind of matter is called a pure substance.[chip: PURE SUBSTANCE]
The distilled water is a pure substance, and the juice is not.[tick on the water box; cross on the juice box]
In a copper wire, every particle is a copper particle, so the wire is a pure substance.[copper wire; box of filled circles; tick]
In table sugar, every particle is a sugar particle, so sugar is a pure substance.[sugar; box of filled circles; tick]
Sea water looks as clear, but it holds water particles and salt particles.[clear sea water; box with filled and open circles; cross]
That is two kinds of matter, so sea water is not a pure substance.[verdict: NOT A PURE SUBSTANCE]
So, here's what we've seen. A sample that contains only one kind of matter is called a pure substance.[the rule slide: the water box and the sea water box with their tick and cross; chip PURE SUBSTANCE; nothing else printed]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “A sample that contains only one kind of matter is called a pure substance.”]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

video

Sea water looks as clear as distilled water, but zoomed in it holds water particles and salt particles. Is sea water a pure substance?

  1. A Yes, it is a pure substance.
  2. B No, it is not a pure substance.
Answer

Key: B — No, it is not a pure substance.

A pure substance holds one kind of matter only.
Sea water holds water particles and salt particles: two kinds.
So sea water is not a pure substance, however clear it looks.

Feedback per option

  • A Two kinds of particle mingled means more than one kind of matter.
  • B Water particles and salt particles are two kinds of matter, so sea water is not a pure substance.

More than one kind: a mixture

MAT-028 · Mixture — Classify a sample as a pure substance or a mixture based on whether it contains more than one substance.

Script
SayScreen
A student pours salt into a bowl of sand and stirs.[a bowl of sand; salt pours in; a spoon stirs]
The bowl now holds one gray-white heap that looks like one material.[the heap; label ONE HEAP?]
Under a magnifying glass, she still sees salt grains and sand grains side by side.[magnifying glass: clear grains and dark grains]
Is the heap one kind of matter, or two?[question mark]
It is two kinds of matter.[chip: TWO KINDS]
Salt on its own is a pure substance: every particle is a salt particle.[box of filled circles labeled SALT]
Zoomed in, salt particles and sand particles sit side by side in the heap, each still itself.[sample box in clumps: filled sand particles and open salt particles, salt grains here and sand grains there]
A sample that contains two or more substances mingled together is called a mixture.[chip: MIXTURE]
Stirring does not turn two substances into one; each keeps its own particles.[the spoon stirs; the two kinds stay two kinds]
Trail mix is nuts, raisins and seeds in one bowl, so trail mix is a mixture you can see.[bowl of trail mix; tick]
Distilled water is one kind of particle only, so it is a pure substance, not a mixture.[box of filled circles; cross]
Air holds several gases mingled together, so air is a mixture you cannot see.[box with filled and open circles labeled AIR; tick]
So, here's what we've seen. A sample that contains two or more substances mingled together is called a mixture.[the rule slide: the heap box and the trail mix; chip MIXTURE; nothing else printed]
So the heap is a mixture.[the heap box; verdict A MIXTURE]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “A sample that contains two or more substances mingled together is called a mixture.”]

A mixture can be taken apart

MAT-029 · Mixtures can be separated by physical means — State that the components of a mixture can be separated because each keeps its own properties.

Script
SayScreen
A student spills iron filings into a tray of sand.[a tray of sand; dark filings scatter into it]
Picking the filings out one by one would take all day.[a hand with tweezers; a clock]
She holds a magnet just above the tray.[a bar magnet lowers over the tray]
The filings jump up onto the magnet, and the sand stays in the tray.[filings fly up and cling to the magnet; the sand stays]
Why could the magnet pull one part out and leave the other?[question mark]
The iron stayed magnetic in the tray, and the sand did not.[chip: IRON STILL MAGNETIC]
The tray holds iron filings and sand mingled together, so it is a mixture.[chip: A MIXTURE]
Zoomed in, iron particles and sand particles sit side by side, each still itself.[sample box in clumps: filled sand particles, open iron particles gathered in small clumps, each kind still itself]
Iron on its own is magnetic; sand on its own is not.[a magnet over iron: pulls; over sand: nothing]
In the mixture, the iron is still magnetic, and the sand is still not.[the same two tests inside the tray]
So in a mixture, each substance keeps its own properties.[chip: EACH PART KEEPS ITS OWN PROPERTIES]
The magnet pulls on the iron and not on the sand, so it lifts only the iron.[the filings rise to the magnet; the sand stays]
Other mixtures come apart the same way: in water, sawdust floats and gravel sinks.[bucket: sawdust on top, gravel on the bottom]
And from sugar water, the water evaporates, but the sugar stays.[dish; the water leaves as a gas; sugar grains remain]
Here's the pattern. In a mixture each substance keeps its own properties, so the parts of a mixture can be separated.[the rule slide: the tray and the magnet with its filings; chip EACH PART KEEPS ITS OWN PROPERTIES; nothing else printed]
Okay, now your turn.[the rule slide's images hold; closing screen: one line — “In a mixture each substance keeps its own properties, so the parts of a mixture can be separated.”]
