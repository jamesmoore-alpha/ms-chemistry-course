Three kinds of particle inside every atom

MAT-049 · Atoms contain three kinds of subatomic particle — Name the three subatomic particles found in every atom.

Script
SayScreen
A pencil leaves a dark mark on paper, and the dark stuff in the mark is carbon.[a pencil draws a mark; label CARBON]
Carbon is an element, so every atom in the mark is a carbon atom.[a zoom circle opens: a box of gray circles]
One carbon atom is the smallest piece that is still carbon, yet scientists can take an atom apart. What is inside?[one gray circle lifted out; question mark]
Let's zoom in on one gray circle and open it up.[zoom lines; the circle becomes a large pale disc that fills the frame]
Inside it are much smaller particles, of three kinds.[the cluster and the dots appear inside the disc]
The filled circles marked plus are called protons.[chip: PROTON, pointer to a filled circle]
The open circles with no mark are called neutrons.[chip: NEUTRON, pointer to an open circle]
The small dark dots marked minus are called electrons.[chip: ELECTRON, pointer to a dot]
No proton, neutron or electron is carbon; carbon is what the whole atom is.[the whole disc outlined, label CARBON ATOM]
Now consider an atom of helium. It holds the same three kinds, with fewer of each.[the helium atom beside the carbon atom; 2, 2, 2 against 6, 6, 6]
So, every atom contains three kinds of smaller particle: protons, neutrons and electrons.[the rule slide: the carbon atom and the helium atom, opened; chips PROTON, NEUTRON, ELECTRON; nothing else printed]
An atom of carbon and an atom of helium contain the same three kinds; only the numbers differ.[the two atoms with their counts]
So if you take one carbon atom from the pencil mark apart, you find those three kinds, not smaller pieces of carbon.[the mark, the one circle, the opened circle]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: one line — "Every atom contains three kinds of smaller particle: protons, neutrons and electrons."]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

video

  1. A three kinds of smaller particle
  2. B smaller and smaller pieces of carbon

Key: A — three kinds of smaller particle

Every atom contains three kinds of smaller particle.
They are protons, neutrons and electrons.
None of them is carbon.

  • B None of the three kinds is carbon; carbon is what the whole atom is.
  • C The opened-up atom holds particles: protons, neutrons and electrons.

Where each particle sits, and its charge

MAT-050 · Charge and location of each subatomic particle — State the charge and location of protons, neutrons, and electrons in an atom.

Script
SayScreen
Here is the inside of a lithium atom, a soft, silvery metal.[the lithium drawing builds: rings, then the cluster, then the dots]
Some particles sit packed in the middle.[the middle highlighted]
Others sit out on the rings.[the rings highlighted]
Some particles carry a plus mark, some a minus mark, and some no mark. Which particle sits where?[the marks flash; question mark]
In the middle, the protons and the neutrons sit packed together.[the cluster grows: 3 filled, 4 open]
This tiny packed center of the atom is called the nucleus.[a dashed ring round the cluster; chip: NUCLEUS]
The electrons sit on the rings, outside the nucleus.[the 3 dots appear on the rings; label ELECTRONS, OUTSIDE]
Now consider a wool sweater pulled off over your head. Your hair stands on end, because rubbing gave the hair a charge.[a sweater comes off; hair stands up; chip: CHARGE]
Charge comes in two kinds, positive and negative, and one of each cancels the other.[a + and a − slide together and fade]
So what do the marks mean?[the marks flash again]
Each proton carries a positive charge: that is its plus mark.[a proton enlarged, its + glows]
Each electron carries a negative charge.[a dot with its −]
A neutron carries no charge at all.[an open circle with nothing]
The drawing is a model: the real atom is mostly empty space, and electrons do not run on tracks.[the rings fade to faint; label MODEL]
So, protons and neutrons sit together in a tiny center called the nucleus.[the rule slide: the labeled lithium atom; chip NUCLEUS; nothing else printed]
Protons carry a positive charge; neutrons carry no charge.[a proton's plus glows; an open circle with nothing]
Electrons move around the outside of the nucleus and carry a negative charge.[the dots on the rings, minus marks glowing]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: one line — “Protons and neutrons sit together in a tiny center called the nucleus.”]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

  2. B the charge

Key: C — the nucleus

Protons and neutrons sit packed together in the middle of the atom.
That tiny packed center is called the nucleus.

  • B A charge is what a proton or an electron carries, not a place in the atom.
  • C The tiny packed center, holding the protons and neutrons, is called the nucleus.

The proton count names the element

MAT-051 · The number of protons identifies the element — State which element an atom is given its number of protons.

Script
SayScreen
Here are two atoms drawn side by side, and they look almost the same.[the two atoms, unlabeled, side by side]
The left atom is carbon, the soot on a burnt match.[a burnt match; label CARBON under the left atom]
The right atom is nitrogen, the gas that makes up most of the air.[the sky; label NITROGEN under the right atom]
So what inside them tells them apart?[question mark between the atoms]
The number of protons does.[the protons in both atoms glow]
Count the protons in the left atom: 6 protons. Every atom with 6 protons is a carbon atom.[the six protons counted off; label CARBON]
Count the protons in the right atom: 7 protons. Every atom with 7 protons is a nitrogen atom.[the seven protons counted off; label NITROGEN]
Only the protons were counted; the neutrons were not.[chips: 6 PROTONS carbon / 7 PROTONS nitrogen; the neutrons dimmed]
Here are the first ten elements, from 1 proton to 10 protons.[the ten-row table slides in: proton count beside element name]
Find the count in the table and read off the element: 8 protons is oxygen.[row 8 highlighted]
And 4 protons is beryllium, 9 protons is fluorine, 2 protons is helium.[rows 4, 9 and 2 highlighted in turn; the three atoms with their names]
So, the number of protons decides which element an atom is.[the rule slide: the two atoms and the table; nothing else printed]
Count the protons, find that number in the table, and read off the element.[a finger traces row 7 across]
So in the two atoms, 6 protons make carbon and 7 protons make nitrogen; the proton count tells them apart.[the two atoms, labeled]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: one line — "The number of protons decides which element an atom is."]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

Change the proton count, change the element

MAT-051b · Changing the proton count changes the element — Explain why adding or removing a proton from an atom changes it into a different element.

Script
SayScreen
Imagine a carbon atom with 6 protons in its nucleus.[the carbon atom; its six protons counted off]
Now imagine one more proton is pushed in: 7 protons. Is it still carbon?[a proton slides in; the count reads 7; question mark]
No, the atom is not carbon anymore.[label CARBON fades; question mark stays]
Here is the carbon atom, 6 protons, and one proton goes into the nucleus.[before drawing; the loose proton with its arrow]
Count again: 7 protons. Row 7 in the table says nitrogen.[after drawing; the seven protons counted off; row 7 lit]
So the atom is now nitrogen, not carbon.[the label switches CARBON → NITROGEN; chip: 7 PROTONS nitrogen]
Now consider an oxygen atom with 8 protons, and one proton comes out.[the oxygen atom; a proton drawn out along an arrow]
Count again: 7 protons, so the oxygen atom has become a nitrogen atom.[the count reads 7; label OXYGEN → NITROGEN]
Now imagine a neutron is added to a carbon atom instead.[the carbon atom; an open circle slides in]
The proton count is still 6, so the atom is still carbon.[the six protons counted off; label STILL CARBON]
Changing the proton count takes enormous force, so it happens inside stars.[a star]
On a bench, a carbon atom stays carbon.[a bench, label STAYS CARBON]
So, change the proton count, and you change the element.[the rule slide: the carbon-to-nitrogen pair; nothing else printed]
Adding or removing a neutron leaves the element the same.[the neutron pair; label STILL CARBON]
So the carbon atom with one extra proton has 7 protons, and 7 protons make it nitrogen.[the after drawing, labeled NITROGEN]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: one line — “Change the proton count, and you change the element.”]

Protons and electrons balance in a neutral atom

MAT-052 · A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons — Work out the number of electrons in a neutral atom given its number of protons.

Script
SayScreen
An atom of oxygen has 8 protons, and each proton carries a positive charge.[the oxygen atom; its eight protons counted off, plus marks glowing]
Yet an ordinary oxygen atom has no overall charge at all. Something must cancel the positives.[label: NO OVERALL CHARGE; question mark]
How many electrons does the atom have?[the rings, empty, with a question mark]
It has 8 electrons.[8 dots appear faintly on the rings]
Each proton carries one positive charge, each electron carries one negative charge, and a neutron carries no charge.[three chips: PROTON +, ELECTRON −, NEUTRON no charge]
One positive charge and one negative charge cancel each other out.[a + and a − slide together and fade]
The oxygen atom's 8 protons carry 8 positive charges.[tally: 8 plus marks]
To cancel 8 positive charges, the atom needs 8 negative charges, so it needs 8 electrons.[8 minus marks line up under the pluses; the 8 dots on the rings brighten]
8 positive charges and 8 negative charges cancel, so the atom has no overall charge.[the pairs fade; label NO OVERALL CHARGE]
An atom with no overall charge is called a neutral atom.[chip: NEUTRAL ATOM]
In a neutral atom the number of electrons equals the number of protons.[electrons = protons]
Now consider a neutral lithium atom: 3 protons, so 3 electrons.[the lithium atom with its counts]
And a neutral neon atom: 10 protons, so 10 electrons.[the neon atom with its counts]
So, in a neutral atom the positive charges and the negative charges cancel, and the number of electrons equals the number of protons.[the rule slide: the oxygen tally, 8 pluses and 8 minuses; chip NEUTRAL ATOM; nothing else printed]
So the oxygen atom has 8 protons and 8 positive charges, and 8 electrons cancel them.[the complete oxygen atom, 8 dots on the rings]
Okay, now your turn.[closing screen: one line — "In a neutral atom the number of electrons equals the number of protons."]

“Okay, now your turn.” — the attached question

video

An oxygen atom has 8 protons and no overall charge. How many electrons does it have?

  1. A 0
  2. B 8
  3. C 16
Answer

Key: B — 8

Each proton carries one positive charge: 8 positives.
Each electron carries one negative charge, and one of each cancels.
So the neutral oxygen atom has 8 electrons.

Feedback per option

  • A With no electrons the 8 positive charges would not be cancelled.
  • B 8 positive charges need 8 negative charges, so 8 electrons.
  • C The neutrons carry no charge, so only the 8 protons need cancelling: 8 electrons.

Which particle inside an atom carries a negative charge?

  1. A a proton
  2. B a neutron
  3. C an electron
Answer

Key: C — an electron

Protons carry a positive charge; neutrons carry no charge.
Electrons carry a negative charge.

Feedback per option

  • A A proton carries a positive charge.
  • B A neutron carries no charge.
  • C An electron carries a negative charge and moves around the outside of the nucleus.
video

An atom has 8 protons in its nucleus. Which element is it?

  1. A carbon
  2. B nitrogen
  3. C oxygen
Answer

Key: C — oxygen

The number of protons decides which element an atom is.
Every atom with 8 protons is oxygen.

Feedback per option

  • A Every atom with 6 protons is carbon.
  • B Every atom with 7 protons is nitrogen.
  • C Every atom with 8 protons is oxygen: the proton count decides the element.
video

A neutral nitrogen atom has 7 protons. How many electrons does it have?

  1. A 0
  2. B 7
  3. C 14
Answer

Key: B — 7

In a neutral atom the positive and negative charges cancel.
So the number of electrons equals the number of protons.
7 protons, so 7 electrons.

Feedback per option

  • A With no electrons the 7 positive charges would not be cancelled.
  • B In a neutral atom the number of electrons equals the number of protons: 7.
  • C The neutrons carry no charge and need no cancelling; 7 protons need 7 electrons.
