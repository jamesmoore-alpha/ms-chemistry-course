What the reviews kept finding, and what changed because of it

Every topic went through the same six readers before I touched it, and the same faults kept coming back.

Each one is now a rule in the vault, a step in the skill that would have caught it, and where possible a

mechanical sweep. The three that mattered most:

  • Test items were lesson checks with the nouns changed. In every bank expansion the non-author review

found four to thirteen "renamed templates" the author's own grep had missed. Fix: every item is designed

from a move line first and read against an inventory of every check; a course-wide register of every

named student (287 names) is built by script, not by hand.

  • Figures handed over the answer. Hidden drawing descriptions named the sort ("a metal"), headache

figures answered their own question, a rule table sat beside the sort item it answers, blue litmus was

drawn so light it read as a cut-off strip. Fix: the hidden description gives drawn data only, the

headache figure is the teaching figure with the answer's layer off, and every render is measured by

script (thumbnail renderers hid text that a browser clipped).

  • Later items gave earlier ones away. Term-recall items printed their answer in the stems below them;

bank items stated in a key the fact another item tests. Fix: term-recall items sit last (pending your

delivery ruling below); every bank carries a draw-one-per-test list the PP100 draw must respect.

Decisions waiting on you

Each was taken at the blueprint's recommendation or the coordinator's default so the build could finish.

None blocks a student sitting the course; each changes what a small pass would redo.

1. How the platform shows a quiz — one item at a time, or the page?

Call assumed: one at a time. Evidence: with a whole quiz on one screen, either the term-recall item

prints its answer below (old order) or the fluency stems print it above (new order); two Topic 12

give-aways and every topic's term-item position hang on this. What changes: nothing if one at a time;

if the page, term items move to a separate screen or become MCQs.

2. Before-and-after drawings: conserve atom counts everywhere, or keep nine particles a box?

Call assumed: nine a box in Topics 7 and 11 (atom KINDS always conserved), counts conserved from Topic 12

on, and no stem ever points at the count. Evidence: Topic 12 teaches conservation; a student who counts

Topic 11's boxes sees 4 become 9. What changes: redraw ~15 Topic 7/11 pairs if you want counts everywhere.

3. Phone-width tables. Call assumed: accept. Evidence: a 540-unit table renders its 13 px text at

about 8 px in a 324 px column, true of every table since Topic 3. What changes: one page-builder CSS rule

(horizontal scroll on narrow screens) if you want it.

4. Blueprint decisions for Topics 4–14 were all taken at their recommendation (recorded per topic in

topicNN_build/records/ADJUDICATION_*.md §6). Two worth a look: Topic 14 teaches bases on washing soda

solution (baking soda solution is borderline for litmus) and says "slaked lime" for the acid-lake line.

5. Number of forms. Call assumed: one Form A per checkpoint and one end-of-course Form E of 40. The

architecture leaves five forms as your call; the banks (60 each) support it.

6. Small register calls: steel "about 8 g/cm³" (Topic 4's value; 7.8 the alternative); the litmus

scope line in Topic 14 L04 (litmus changes only below ~4.5 or above ~8.3 — one bank item stands or falls

with it); Topic 6 says a more concentrated drink "tastes stronger" in three places, which seeds the

concentrated-equals-strong confusion Topic 14 later avoids; the register stamps TEKS 6.6/7.6/8.6, so the

end-of-course weighting uses those codes.

7. Two rule promotions in the vault: the headache-figure rule is ready to go from provisional to

standing (a second course now uses it); the S28–S34 sweeps are WARN-only and could be promoted to FAIL.
